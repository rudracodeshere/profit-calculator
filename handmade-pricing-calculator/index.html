How the formula works

Handmade pricing starts from your base cost: everything one item uses up, including your time. Materials are the obvious part. Labor is minutes times an hourly rate you would actually accept. Overhead is the monthly stuff that isn't in any one item (tools, software, studio rent, the craft fair table) divided by how many items you make in a month.

Base cost = materials + (minutes / 60 x hourly rate) + overhead per item
Wholesale = base cost x 2  |  Retail = wholesale x 2

The first doubling is your profit as a business, separate from your wage. The second doubling is the shop's margin when a store buys from you wholesale. If you sell direct, that second half pays for the selling: platform fees, photos, customer messages and the items that don't sell.

A worked example

A set of handmade soap dishes uses $6 of clay and glaze and takes 30 minutes. At $20 an hour, your time is $10.00. Add $1.50 of overhead and the base cost is $17.50. Double it for a wholesale price of $35.00 ($17.50 profit per piece to a shop). Double again for retail: $70.00.

Sold on Etsy at $70.00, the fees are $7.10, so you keep $62.90. After your $17.50 base cost, $45.40 is profit on top of your wage. On Shopify Basic the per-sale fee is $2.33 and the profit is $50.17, but the monthly plan fee comes out of that, so it only wins once you have enough sales.

When the numbers look too high

If the retail price feels steep, don't cut your hourly rate first. Look at the time instead: batching steps, making in larger runs and simplifying the design all lower the base cost without paying yourself less. If you will never sell wholesale, you can price retail lower than 4x cost, but keep the gap. The fees, the free shipping you build into the price and the occasional sale all come out of it.

Questions

How do you calculate the price of a handmade item?

Add materials, your time at an hourly rate and a share of overhead to get the base cost. Double it for wholesale and double again for retail. With $6 materials, 30 minutes at $20 an hour and $1.50 overhead, that is $17.50, then $35.00 wholesale and $70.00 retail.

Should I include my labor in the cost?

Yes. If your time is not in the base cost, the profit you see is really your wage, and you can't tell whether a product is worth making. Pay yourself in the cost, then the multiplier is profit on top.

How do I work out overhead per item?

Add up the monthly costs that aren't in any one product (tools, software, rent, booth fees) and divide by how many items you make in a month. $60 a month over 40 items is $1.50 per item.

Do I need a wholesale price if I only sell on Etsy?

It helps. Pricing retail at twice wholesale means a shop can stock you later without you underpricing yourself, and it leaves room for Etsy's fees, which take $7.10 of a $70.00 sale.
