How the 2026 1099-K test works

Payment apps and online marketplaces (the IRS calls them third party settlement organizations) have to file a Form 1099-K for you only if, in one calendar year, they paid you over $20,000 for goods or services and you had over 200 transactions with them. Both conditions must be met, and both say "over": exactly $20,000.00, or exactly 200 transactions, is not enough.

The test runs separately at each platform. Etsy looks only at what Etsy paid you, eBay only at what eBay paid you. Your sales do not add up across platforms for the 1099-K, even though they all count on your tax return.

Federal 1099-K from a platform = what it paid you > $20,000 AND your transactions on it > 200

Worked example

You sell on Etsy and eBay and live in a state with no lower rule. Etsy paid you $14,500 for 240 sales in 2026. 240 is over 200, but $14,500 is not over $20,000, so Etsy files no federal 1099-K. It would take $5,500.01 more to go over the line.

eBay paid you $6,200 for 85 sales. Both tests fail: $13,800.01 more needed to go over $20,000, and 116 more transactions needed to go over 200.

Combined, that is $20,700.00 across 325 transactions. The total is over $20,000, yet no federal 1099-K arrives, because each platform is tested on its own. You can document $9,850 of fees, refunds, shipping and item costs, so you report the full $20,700 and subtract those costs: $20,700 minus $9,850 = $10,850.00 before any other deductions.

Now pick Massachusetts ($600 or more): both platforms would likely send a 1099-K under the state rule. Vermont ($2,000 or more) gives the same result.

What changed, and where $600 and $2,000 come from

$600 is gone at the federal level. The American Rescue Plan Act of 2021 lowered the line to more than $600 with no transaction count, and the IRS delayed it with transition relief (Notices 2023-10, 2023-74 and 2024-85). The One, Big, Beautiful Bill then retroactively reinstated the old test of over $20,000 and over 200 transactions (IRS Fact Sheet FS-2025-08). Posts that say the 1099-K line "dropped to $600 in 2026" are wrong.

$2,000 is a different form. Starting with 2026, the federal minimum for certain other information returns, such as Form 1099-NEC for contractor pay and parts of Form 1099-MISC, rose from $600 to $2,000 (IRS Publication 1099). It does not change the 1099-K test. Some states tie their own 1099-K line to that amount, which is why Vermont now says $2,000. Maryland and Virginia use the same link, so their lines may move from $600 to $2,000 as well.

Why you might still get a 1099-K below $20,000

Your state has a lower line. Platforms send a form to payees in states such as Illinois, Massachusetts, Maryland, Virginia and Vermont at much lower amounts. The IRS says a state rule "could result in you receiving a Form 1099-K, even if the total gross payments and transactions did not exceed the federal reporting threshold."

Card payments through your own processor. The IRS sets no threshold for payment card transactions: if you received even $0.01 of payment card payments, it says you should get a 1099-K. Processors do not all follow this in practice (Square, for one, says it uses the $20,000 and 200 test), so check your processor's help page.

Backup withholding. A platform that withheld tax from your payouts (usually because your tax ID was missing or wrong) must file a 1099-K for you whatever the totals. PayPal and Venmo say they issue one for anyone subject to backup withholding.

The platform chose to. The IRS notes that a platform may still send a 1099-K for amounts under the federal line.

Why the 1099-K number is bigger than your profit

Box 1a is the gross amount paid. The IRS says it "doesn't include adjustments for fees, credits, refunds, shipping, cash equivalents or discounts," and that you can deduct those items when you report the income. It also ignores what the items cost you.

Platforms do not all count gross the same way. Etsy says its gross may include shipping, refunds, card processing fees, sales tax applied with its sales tax tool and canceled orders. Mercari includes buyer-paid shipping and sales tax but leaves out the buyer's service and payment processing fees and canceled or refunded transactions. PayPal counts only goods and services payments, not payments between friends and family. So two sellers with the same sales can see different 1099-K totals, and neither number is your profit.

Whether or not a form arrives, the income goes on your return. Keep your sales exports, fee statements and receipts so you can get from the gross figure down to what you actually made.

Questions

What is the 1099-K threshold for 2026?

Over $20,000 in payments AND over 200 transactions from one platform or payment app in the calendar year (IRS Publication 1099 (2026) and the Instructions for Form 1099-K). The One, Big, Beautiful Bill retroactively reinstated this test, so the $600 rule is gone. Forms for 2026 are due to you by January 31, 2027, which is a Sunday, so the deadline moves to Monday, February 1, 2027. Some states use a lower line.

If I don't get a 1099-K, do I still report the income?

Yes. The IRS says "All income, no matter the amount, is taxable unless the tax law says it isn't", "even if you don't get a Form 1099-K." Selling a used personal item for less than you paid is a separate case: the IRS says that sale does not add to your taxable income, though you should keep records of what you paid.

Do my Etsy, eBay and PayPal sales add up toward $20,000?

No. Each platform files based only on what it paid you. $14,500 on Etsy plus $6,200 on eBay means no federal 1099-K from either, even though the total is $20,700.00. You still report all $20,700.00. Etsy does combine your Etsy shops under one SSN or EIN.

Why did I get a 1099-K (or why is it so high) when I made less?

A lower state threshold, card payments taken through your own card processor (the IRS sets no threshold for these), or backup withholding at a platform such as PayPal or Venmo can each trigger a form. The amount is high because box 1a is gross, before fees, refunds and shipping. The IRS says you can deduct those items when you report the income, so subtract the costs you can document.
