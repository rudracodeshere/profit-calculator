How Whatnot fees work

Commission. Whatnot takes a percentage of the item's sale price, after any coupon you gave. Shipping and sales tax are not included. Since September 21, 2026 the rate depends on two things: your tier, set by your total sales in all categories over the last 4-week period, and the category of the sale. For a live sale that is the show's category; for a marketplace sale it is the product's category. The Standard rate is 8% (4% for coins), and it drops as your 4-week sales grow.

Payment processing. 2.9% + $0.30 per order in the US, charged on everything the buyer pays: the item, the shipping and the sales tax. Tiers do not lower it. On cheap items this is the fee that hurts, because the $0.30 and the 2.9% on shipping stay the same however low the bid goes.

Shipping. Buyers pay shipping by default, and that money buys the label, so it is not income. When the item is taxable, 29 states and DC generally charge the buyer sales tax on that shipping too, and processing is charged on that tax as well. Some sellers can offer free shipping in their shows, or cap what buyers pay and cover the rest; the part you cover comes out of your payout. Whatnot's own example: with a $7.75 cap and a $20 shipping cost, the buyer pays $7.75 and you pay $12.25.

Sales tax on fees. Whatnot says a few states make it charge sales tax on its fees, which it takes from your earnings. Commission is taxed for sellers in DC, Hawaii, New Mexico and West Virginia. Processing is taxed in Connecticut (at a special 1%), Hawaii, New Mexico, Ohio, South Dakota, Texas (your full sales tax rate cut by 20%, so Houston's 8.25% becomes 6.6%) and West Virginia. Resale certificates don't exempt you. Enter your rate under Edit fee rates.

Cancellation fee. If you cancel an order for a reason in your control, Whatnot may charge $3 or 3% of the order total, whichever is higher. You see whether a fee applies before you confirm. Your first one is waived, and accepting a buyer's cancellation request made through Whatnot carries no fee.

The formula

Whatnot pays you = item price - commission rate x item price - (2.9% x (item + shipping + tax) + $0.30) - any state tax on those fees - shipping you cover

Profit is that payout minus what the item cost you. The break-even winning bid is the lowest price where profit reaches zero, after the commission and processing that price itself adds.

Worked example

You sell a trading card for $25 at the Standard tier (8%). The buyer pays $6 shipping plus 8% sales tax on the item and the shipping ($2.48), so the buyer pays $33.48. Commission is 8% of $25 = $2.00. Processing is 2.9% of $33.48 + $0.30 = $1.27. Total fees are $3.27, 13.1% of the sale price, and Whatnot pays you $21.73. After a $10 item cost your profit is $11.73, a 46.9% margin. The break-even winning bid is $11.80: at that price the fees come to $1.80 and your profit is exactly $0.00.

At Tier 3 (7.25%) the same card pays $1.81 commission, so fees are $3.08, you get $21.92 and keep $11.92. The break-even bid drops to $11.70.

A $1 start that sells for $1, with the same $6 shipping: the buyer pays $7.56, commission is $0.08 and processing $0.52, so fees are $0.60, 60.0% of the sale. If the item cost you $0.50, you lose $0.10. That item needs a $1.11 bid just to break even.

A $2,000 card with the high-value promo: the buyer pays $2,166.48 with tax. Commission is 8% of the first $1,500 = $120.00, the same figure as Whatnot's own example, and processing on the full $2,166.48 is $63.13. Whatnot pays you $1,816.87, a $616.87 profit after a $1,200 cost. Without the promo the commission would be $160.00.

The same $25 sale as a coin (4%) by a seller in Houston, Texas who also covers $2 of shipping: commission $1.00, processing $1.27 plus $0.08 sales tax on it at 6.6% (Houston's 8.25% cut by 20%, the rate in Whatnot's own example), so Whatnot pays $20.65 and the profit is $10.65.

Tips for Whatnot sellers

On $1 starts, the real cost is not the commission. It is the $0.30 plus 2.9% of the shipping and tax, which you pay even when the bid stays at $1. Know your break-even bid before you run an item at $1.

A tier you reach this period only pays off next period, and every period starts again from zero. If you are close to a threshold near the end of a period, the extra sales can lower your rates for the whole next 4 weeks.

The headline 3% rate is a custom deal for the very largest sellers who work with an account manager. It is not on the public rate card. And don't list in a cheaper category than what you sell: Whatnot treats that as fee avoidance.

Questions

How much does Whatnot take from a $25 sale?

$3.27 if the buyer pays $6 shipping and 8% sales tax on the item and shipping, at the Standard 8% rate: $2.00 commission on the item plus $1.27 processing (2.9% of the $33.48 the buyer pays, plus $0.30). That is 13.1% of the sale price. For a trading card or sports item at Tier 3 (7.25%) it drops to $3.08.

Does Whatnot charge fees on shipping and sales tax?

Commission, no: it is charged on the item price only, after any seller coupon. Processing, yes: 2.9% + $0.30 is charged on the full amount the buyer pays, including shipping and sales tax. On a $25 card with $6 shipping and $2.48 tax, processing is charged on $33.48.

How do Whatnot's commission tiers work?

Your total sales across all categories in a 4-week (28-day) period set your tier for the next period. The US thresholds are $15,000 for Tier 1, $35,000 for Tier 2, $50,000 for Tier 3, $100,000 for Tier 4, $250,000 for Tier 5 and $500,000 for Tier 6. Your rate is locked for the whole period, and each sale is charged its category's rate at your tier. Cancelled orders, private shows and local pickup orders come off the total. Rates as low as 3% are custom deals for the largest sellers.

What is the $1,500 high-value commission break?

Since January 14, 2026, Whatnot charges 0% commission on the part of an item's sale price above $1,500 in Comics & Anime, Toys & Hobbies, Coins & Money, Trading Card Games, Entertainment Cards, the Sports Singles subcategories, Bags & Accessories and Jewelry & Watches (the last two were added June 4, 2026). Sports breaks and Surprise Products are excluded, and processing is still charged on the full order. A $2,000 sale at 8% pays $120 commission instead of $160. Whatnot's example uses 8% on the first $1,500; the calculator applies your selected rate there, and Seller Hub shows the exact charge. It is a limited-time promotion.
