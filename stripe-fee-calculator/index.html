Stripe's standard card fee

On standard US pricing, Stripe charges 2.9% + $0.30 on each successful card payment. Two surcharges stack on top: 1.5% when the card was issued outside the US, and 1% when the payment has to be converted into your payout currency. An international card in a foreign currency costs 5.4% + $0.30.

Payout = amount - (amount x rate + $0.30)

Example: a $100 domestic payment costs $3.20 and you receive $96.80. If the card is international, the fee is $4.70 and you get $95.30. Add currency conversion and it is $5.70, leaving $94.30.

Charging enough to cover the fee

Passing the fee on means solving for the price, not adding 2.9%. Divide what you want to keep, plus $0.30, by 1 minus the rate:

Charge = (amount you want + $0.30) / (1 - rate)

To receive $100 from a domestic card, charge $103.30. For an international card, charge $104.92. Check your local rules before adding a surcharge to card payments; some US states restrict it.

Your monthly Stripe cost

Stripe has no monthly fee on standard pricing, so your cost scales with volume. 500 payments of $40 is $20,000 a month, and Stripe takes $730.00 of it, an effective 3.65%. The $0.30 fixed part is $150 of that, which is why low-priced products or many small payments push your effective rate up. If 100% of those payments were on international cards, the bill would be $1,030.00.

Questions

How much does Stripe charge on $100?

$3.20 for a domestic card at 2.9% + $0.30, so you receive $96.80. An international card costs $4.70.

How much should I charge to get $100 after Stripe fees?

$103.30 with a US card. With an international card, $104.92.

What is the Stripe fee on $50?

$1.75 at 2.9% + $0.30, so you receive $48.25.

Does Stripe charge a monthly fee?

Not on standard pricing. You pay per successful payment only. Extra products such as Billing, Tax or Radar for Fraud Teams are priced separately.
