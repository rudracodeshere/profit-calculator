How the late fee is worked out

Most freelancers and small businesses charge a late fee as a monthly percentage of the unpaid balance, often 1.5% a month (18% a year), sometimes with a small flat fee on top. Charging it daily from the due date means the client pays for exactly how late they are, and it gives them a reason to pay today instead of next week.

Late fee = invoice amount x monthly rate x days late / 30 + flat fee

Worked example

A $2,400 invoice is 45 days late. Your terms say 1.5% a month plus a $25 late fee. The percentage part is 2,400 x 0.015 x 45 / 30 = $54.00. Add the $25 flat fee and the late fee is $79.00, so the client now owes $2,479.00. Each extra day adds 2,400 x 0.015 / 30 = $1.20.

With a 15-day grace period the percentage only runs for 30 days: 2,400 x 0.015 x 30 / 30 = $36.00, or $61.00 with the flat fee.

Making a late fee stick

The fee has to be agreed before the work starts. Put it in your contract or proposal, print the wording above on every invoice, and mention it in your reminders from the first overdue email. A fee you add without warning is easy for a client to refuse, and it sours the relationship.

Keep the rate reasonable. In the US the legal maximum depends on your state; some cap late fees or interest well below 18% a year. In the UK, business-to-business invoices carry statutory interest of 8% a year over the Bank of England base rate, plus fixed compensation of £40, £70 or £100 depending on the debt, even if your contract says nothing. In the EU, the Late Payment Directive sets interest at least 8 points over the ECB reference rate plus €40.

The fee is a nudge, not the plan. What gets most invoices paid is a steady sequence of short, polite emails that ask for a specific payment date. The payment reminder email generator writes the right one for how late the invoice is.

Questions

What is a normal late fee on an invoice?

1% to 1.5% a month on the unpaid balance is common, often with a flat fee of $25 to $50. On a $2,400 invoice 45 days late, 1.5% a month comes to $54.00.

Can I charge a late fee if it wasn't in my contract?

Usually not in the US, unless the client agrees to it. In the UK, business invoices carry statutory interest and fixed compensation even without a clause. Add the fee to your terms before the next project.

Is 1.5% a month the same as 18% a year?

Yes, as simple interest: 1.5% x 12 = 18%. This calculator charges it daily, so 45 days at 1.5% a month is 2.25% of the invoice.

When should the late fee start?

The day after the due date, unless your terms give a grace period. A short grace period of 5 to 7 days avoids charging clients whose payment is already in transit.
