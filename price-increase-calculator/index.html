Why a price rise can survive lost sales

When you raise a price, your costs stay the same, so almost all of the increase is extra profit. A product that sells for $20, costs $8 and pays 10% in fees leaves you $10.00 per sale. At $23 it leaves $12.70. The price went up 15%, but the profit per sale went up 27%.

That gap is your cushion. You can sell fewer units and still take home the same amount.

Sales you can lose = 1 - (profit per sale now / profit per sale at new price)

Worked example: raising $20 to $23

You sell 100 a month and make $1,000.00. If sales hold at $23, you make $1,270.00, $270.00 more. To make the same $1,000 at $23 you only need 1,000 / 12.70 = 78.7 sales, so 79. You could lose 21 of your 100 sales, or 21.3%, before the increase costs you anything.

Worked example: cutting $20 to $18

Discounts work the other way. At $18 you keep $8.20 per sale. At 100 sales that is $820.00, $180.00 less than now. To get back to $1,000 you need 1,000 / 8.20 = 122 sales, 22 extra a month or 22.0% more, for a 10% price cut. Check the Etsy sale calculator before you run a discount.

How to test a price change

Change the price on one or two of your steady sellers rather than the whole shop, and leave it for at least a few weeks so normal ups and downs even out. Compare profit, not revenue or order count. A rise that loses fewer sales than this calculator allows has paid off, even if your order count looks worse.

The cushion is biggest on low-margin products, because a few dollars more is a large share of a small profit. Those are also the products where a per-product view from your full sales export is most useful.

Questions

How many customers can I lose if I raise prices?

Divide your profit per sale now by your profit per sale at the new price, and subtract from 1. Going from $10.00 to $12.70 per sale means you can lose 21.3% of sales, about 21 of 100, and earn the same.

How much more do I need to sell after a price cut?

Divide current profit per sale by the new profit per sale. Cutting a $20 price to $18 drops profit from $10.00 to $8.20 per sale, so you need 22.0% more sales just to stand still.

Should I raise prices on Etsy?

Run the numbers for your listing first. Because Etsy's fees are mostly a percentage of the price, a rise keeps about 90% of the extra money. If you would lose fewer sales than the calculator allows, the rise pays.

Does a price increase change my fees?

Yes, percentage fees rise with the price. This calculator includes that: at 10% fees, a $3 rise adds $2.70 of profit per sale, not $3.
