How break-even works

Every sale leaves some money after paying for the product itself and the fees on that sale. That leftover is the contribution per unit. Your fixed costs, the ones you pay whether you sell anything or not, have to be covered by those contributions. Break-even is the point where they are.

Break-even units = fixed costs / (price - variable cost - fees per unit)

Fixed costs for an online seller are things like a Shopify plan, Etsy Plus, email software, design apps, a workspace or a monthly ad budget you have committed to. Variable costs rise with each sale: materials, packaging, the shipping label if you offer free shipping, and marketplace fees.

Worked example

You sell a $24 item that costs $8 to make and ship. Etsy's US fees on it are 9.5% of $24 ($2.28) plus $0.45, so $2.73. Each sale contributes $24 - $8 - $2.73 = $13.27 toward fixed costs.

With $300 a month of fixed costs, you need 300 / 13.27 = 22.6 sales, so 23 sales and $552.00 of revenue to break even. To make $1,000 on top, you need (300 + 1,000) / 13.27 = 97.97, so 98 sales, or $2,352.00 of revenue.

What a 10% price rise does

Raise the price to $26.40 and the contribution goes to $15.44, because nearly all of the extra $2.40 is profit (only the 9.5% fee comes out of it). Break-even falls to 20 sales and the $1,000 goal falls to 85 sales. That is 13 fewer sales for the same profit, which is why a small price change often matters more than a big push for volume. Questions

How do I calculate break-even for a small business?

Add up your monthly fixed costs and divide by what each sale leaves after its own costs and fees. $300 of fixed costs and $13.27 per sale gives 22.6, so 23 sales a month.

Should I include marketplace fees in break-even?

Yes, as a variable cost per unit. Percentage fees grow with the price, so a $24 Etsy sale at 9.5% plus $0.45 carries $2.73 of fees. Monthly plan fees like Shopify or Etsy Plus go in fixed costs instead.

Should I pay myself before break-even?

If you want the business to pay you, put your target pay in the profit goal, or add a salary to fixed costs. Break-even with no pay only means the shop isn't losing cash.

Does raising prices lower break-even?

Yes, often a lot. In the example a 10% rise from $24 to $26.40 lifts contribution from $13.27 to $15.44 and cuts break-even from 23 sales to 20.
