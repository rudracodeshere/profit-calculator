What goes into COGS

Cost of goods sold is what the items you sold cost you to make or buy. For an online seller that usually means materials or wholesale stock, packaging that ships with the product, and inbound shipping or import duties to get supplies to you. Direct labor counts if you pay someone to make the product.

It does not include selling costs: marketplace fees, payment processing, outbound shipping labels, ads or software. Those are expenses, but they belong lower down your profit and loss, and mixing them into COGS makes your gross margin look worse than it is.

COGS per unit from a batch

COGS per unit = (materials + packaging + inbound freight + labor) / units produced

Example: a batch of 60 candles takes $180 of wax, wicks, fragrance and jars, $24 of boxes and labels, and $36 of freight on the supplies. That is $240, or $4.00 per candle. At an $18 price the gross profit is $14.00 per candle, a 77.8% gross margin. If you count 5 hours of your own time at $20, the batch costs $340 and each candle $5.67.

Whether to include your own time is a choice. Leaving it out tells you cash cost. Putting it in tells you whether the price pays you a wage.

COGS for a month, quarter or year

COGS = beginning inventory + purchases - ending inventory

Example: you started the quarter with $2,000 of stock at cost, bought $3,500 more and counted $1,800 left at the end. You sold $3,700 of inventory. On $9,000 of net sales that is $5,300 of gross profit, a 58.9% gross margin.

This method needs an actual count at the end of the period. If you skip it, your COGS will be off by however much stock went missing, broke or was used for samples.

US sole proprietors filing Schedule C will see cost of goods sold on line 4, worked out in Part III. That is only a pointer to where the number goes; the IRS instructions and a tax professional decide what you include.

Questions

Is shipping included in COGS?

Inbound shipping to get stock or materials to you usually is. Outbound shipping labels to customers are a selling cost, not COGS, even though you pay them per order.

Are Etsy or Shopify fees part of cost of goods sold?

No. Marketplace and payment fees are selling expenses. Keep them out of COGS so your gross margin compares products fairly, then subtract fees separately to get net profit.

How do I calculate COGS if I make products myself?

Add up a batch's materials, packaging and inbound freight, then divide by the units it made. $240 for 60 units is $4.00 each. Add labor if you pay a maker or want to price in your own time.

What if I don't track inventory?

Count what you have on hand once at the start and end of the period, valued at what you paid. Then COGS is beginning inventory plus purchases minus ending inventory: $2,000 + $3,500 - $1,800 = $3,700.
