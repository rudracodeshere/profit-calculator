How to set a Christmas budget

Most Christmas overspending isn't the big gifts. It's the costs nobody wrote down: the second trip to the store, the stocking stuffers, the extra groceries for hosting, the postage. So budget the whole season, in two parts:

  • Gifts, per person. Decide an amount for each person before you shop. Grouping helps: kids, close family, then a smaller amount for friends, coworkers, teachers and neighbors.
  • Everything else. Food and hosting, travel, the tree and decorations, wrapping, cards and postage, outings, outfits and giving.

Christmas total = (people x budget each, per group) + food + travel + decorations + wrapping + other

Then work backwards from the day you want to shop. Divide what's still to save by the paydays left, and set that aside every payday. Next year, divide the total by 12 and start in January.

Worked example

Two kids at $150 each, four adults at $75 and five friends and coworkers at $25 come to $300 + $300 + $125 = $725 in gifts. Add $250 for food, $150 for travel, $75 for the tree and decorations and $50 for wrapping and postage: $525. The season costs $1,250.

With $300 already set aside and 8 weeks to go, $950 is left to save. Paid every 2 weeks, that is 4 paydays, so $237.50 a payday (about $118.75 a week). On an $1,800 paycheck that is 13.2%. Saving the same $1,250 from January would take $104.17 a month.

What do people actually spend?

In the National Retail Federation's 2025 holiday survey, US shoppers planned to spend $890.49 on average: $627.93 on gifts and $262.56 on food, candy, decorations and cards. Gallup's October 2025 poll put planned gift spending at $1,007, and $651 for households earning under $50,000. Use these as a sanity check, not a target: the right budget is the one you can pay for in cash by December.

Keeping to it

Write every gift down as you buy it, with what you paid, and check it against that person's budget. When someone goes over, take it from another line on purpose instead of letting the total drift. Keep receipts and return-by dates for anything that might go back. A printable worksheet works; a spreadsheet does the adding up for you.

Questions

How much should I spend on Christmas?

Whatever you can pay in cash by the time you shop. Add up gifts per person plus food, travel, decorations and wrapping, then divide what's left to save by your paydays. If that is more than about 15% of each paycheck, trim the list or the per-person amounts.

How much should I spend per child at Christmas?

There is no standard amount. Pick one figure per child that fits your total, keep it equal between siblings, and count stocking stuffers inside it, not on top.

How much should I save each month for Christmas?

Divide your Christmas total by the months left before you shop. Starting in January, a $1,250 Christmas is $104.17 a month.

What costs do people forget in a Christmas budget?

Food and hosting, travel, wrapping paper, cards and postage, stocking stuffers, outfits for parties, and gifts for teachers, coworkers and neighbors. Give each one its own line.
