How the holiday price is worked out

Holiday price = normal price × (1 + surcharge %) + flat extra

Booking total = regular visits × normal price + holiday visits × holiday price

Only the visits or nights that fall on your holiday dates get the higher price. That keeps a long booking that just touches Christmas from jumping in price for every day.

Worked example

An overnight sitter charges $75 a night and adds 15% on holiday dates. A client books 7 nights, and 3 of them fall on the holiday dates.

The holiday price is $75 × 1.15 = $86.25. The 4 regular nights come to $300 and the 3 holiday nights to $258.75, so the quote is $558.75. That's $33.75 more than 7 nights at the normal price.

With a flat $20 per holiday night instead of a percent, the holiday price is $95 and the quote is $585.

Choosing your surcharge and dates

Percent or flat. A percent grows with your price, so it works across drop-ins, walks and overnights. A flat amount is easier to explain to clients. You can use both.

Pick the dates once and write them down. Common windows are the holiday itself, the days around it, or a set stretch such as December 20 to January 1. Whatever you pick, tell clients when they book so the quote isn't a surprise.

New and existing clients. Some sitters charge regulars a smaller surcharge than new clients. Run the calculator once for each.

Questions

How much should pet sitters charge on holidays?

Many sitters add a surcharge on holiday dates, either a percent of their normal price or a flat amount per visit or night. Use the numbers you would be happy working the holiday for; the calculator shows what each choice does to the quote.

Which days count as holiday dates?

You decide. Some sitters charge holiday rates only on the day itself, others on the days around it, and others on a whole stretch such as December 20 to January 1. Tell clients your dates when they book.

Does the surcharge apply to the whole booking?

Not in this calculator. Only the visits or nights on your holiday dates get the holiday price, and the rest stay at your normal price. In the example, 3 of 7 nights are on holiday dates, so the quote is $558.75 instead of $525.

Can I use a flat holiday fee instead of a percent?

Yes. Set the percent to 0 and enter the flat extra. A $75 night with a $20 flat holiday extra becomes $95.
