The reminder sequence that gets invoices paid


Most late invoices aren't refused, they're forgotten. A short, regular sequence of emails that gets a little firmer each time, and always asks for a specific payment date, gets most of them paid without a single awkward call.

Rules for chasing a late payment


Ask for a date. "When will this be paid?" gets a commitment. "Just checking in" gets ignored.


Reply in the same thread so the client sees every earlier reminder.


Find the person who pays. From the third email, ask who handles invoices and copy them in.


Only name a consequence you will carry out. If you say work pauses at 14 days, pause it. Empty threats teach clients to wait.


Stay polite, even in the final notice. If the debt ever goes to an agency or small claims court, these emails are your evidence.

Questions


How do I politely remind a client to pay an invoice?


Keep it short: the invoice number, amount, due date and payment link, then ask the date it will be paid. For the first overdue reminder, assume it was missed rather than ignored.


How many reminders should I send for an unpaid invoice?


Usually five to seven: before the due date, on it, then at about 3, 7, 14, 30 and 45 days late. Each one gets firmer and the last two name a deadline and what you will do next.


What should a final notice for an unpaid invoice say?


The invoice number, the original due date, the balance including any agreed late fees, a final date to pay, and the step you will take if it isn't paid, such as passing it to a collection agency.


Can I add a late fee to a reminder email?


Only if the client agreed to it before the work started, usually in your contract or on the invoice. If they did, state the fee and the new balance from about 14 days late.
